4/*
5 * gftp_reply.h — client-role FTP control-channel reply parser (RFC 959 §4.2,
6 * RFC 2428 §3) for the outbound gsiftp:// storage driver.
7 *
8 * WHAT: turn raw control-channel bytes into a single completed reply (3-digit
9 * code + final-line text), and decode the two address-bearing replies a client
10 * must interpret to open a data channel — 227 (PASV, IPv4) and 229 (EPSV).
11 *
12 * WHY: phase-82 is the inbound gateway and only ever *emits* replies; a client
13 * must *parse* them, and nothing in the tree did until now. The 227/229 address
14 * decoders are the SSRF-critical seam: a hostile origin can nominate an
15 * arbitrary data-channel address, so these functions bounds-check every octet
16 * and reject malformed input — the caller then screens the extracted address
17 * through net_target.h before dialling. Keeping the parse pure (plain buffers,
18 * no nginx types, no sockets) makes it exhaustively unit-testable off any
19 * server and lets both the blocking driver and any future event client share
20 * one implementation that cannot drift.
21 *
22 * HOW: `gftp_reply_scan` consumes one complete reply from a receive buffer,
23 * handling the multiline `ddd-...\r\n ... \r\nddd ` continuation form; it never
24 * reads past `len` and returns 0 when more bytes are needed (the caller reads
25 * more and rescans). The address decoders operate on the final line's text.
26 */
27
