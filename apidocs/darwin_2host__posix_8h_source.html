1/*
2 * src/platform/darwin/host_posix.h - the Darwin part of the PAL POSIX surface
3 *
4 * WHAT: The Linux spellings the tree is written against, provided on macOS:
5 * Linux-shaped xattr calls with the Linux errno contract, the missing
6 * constants (O_PATH, SOCK_CLOEXEC, MSG_NOSIGNAL, PR_*, CAP_*, ...) and
7 * the openat2(2) ABI the PAL emulates.
8 * HOW: Darwin's xattr calls take (position, options) extras, answer ENOATTR
9 * where Linux says ENODATA (the tree tests ENODATA), and cap names at
10 * XATTR_MAXNAMELEN (127, Linux 255) with ENAMETOOLONG. A name that
11 * cannot exist is simply absent to a reader (the WebDAV dead-property
12 * and lock probes build 170-byte names and must not turn that into a
13 * 500); writes keep ENAMETOOLONG so the caller sees why. The PAL's own
14 * adapters call the host functions as `(getxattr)(...)` to bypass the
15 * call-shape macros. Sharp edge: a function-like macro also captures a
16 * struct member call spelt `driver->getxattr(...)`; write such calls as
17 * `(driver->getxattr)(...)` (a missed one fails to compile here, never
18 * silently; check_platform_leak.py rejects the unparenthesised form).
19 * Each alias keeps the behaviour the tree relied on before the PAL
20 * owned it; the semantic gaps are documented next to each.
21 */

99/* Linux's ENOKEY ("required key not available") has no macOS number. It must
100 * NOT be aliased to EACCES: the two mean opposite things to their callers.
101 * EACCES is the ORIGIN's answer — "you may not have this" — which the cache
102 * fill reports to the client as 403 and treats as definitive. ENOKEY is OUR
103 * failure to answer an auth challenge (the token endpoint was down, say),
104 * which must read as 502: telling a client "you are not allowed" for an image
105 * it is perfectly entitled to pull sends it chasing a permissions problem
106 * that does not exist. A value past ELAST cannot collide with a real errno. */
107#ifndef ENOKEY
108#define ENOKEY (ELAST + 20)
109#endif
