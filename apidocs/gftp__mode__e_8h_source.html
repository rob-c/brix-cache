4/*
5 * gftp_mode_e.h — GridFTP MODE E on the OUTBOUND gsiftp storage driver.
6 *
7 * WHAT: the extended-block receiver (RETR) and sender (STOR) the driver uses
8 * once the store line asks for `mode=e`.
9 *
10 * WHY: MODE S is one byte stream on one socket: a ranged read costs a REST and
11 * a fresh transfer, a whole-file read cannot use more than one TCP connection,
12 * and there is no in-band way for the origin to say where a byte belongs. MODE
13 * E — GFD.020 §3.4, what every production GridFTP mover actually negotiates —
14 * frames the transfer as offset-addressed blocks that may arrive out of order
15 * across up to `Parallelism` data connections. It is the difference between
16 * "we speak the protocol" and "we can pull from a real endpoint at rate".
17 *
18 * WHY IT IS A SECURITY BOUNDARY: because blocks carry their own offsets, a
19 * block that overlaps an already-committed range is not a benign retransmit —
20 * it is either corruption or a deliberate attempt to rewrite bytes the receiver
21 * has already accepted (and, for a fill, already checksummed). The receiver
22 * therefore keeps every committed range and fails the transfer on the first
23 * overlap, exactly as the inbound door does; the shared test is
24 * ftp_eb_range_overlaps() in protocols/gridftp/ftp_eblock.h.
25 *
26 * HOW: the receiver polls all data connections at once, tracking per-connection
27 * header/payload state, and sinks each payload at `offset - start`. It stops
28 * when every connection has sent its EOD and the count promised by the EOF block
29 * has been met. The sender frames each chunk the source hands it at the running
30 * offset and closes with the combined EOF|EOD trailer the door's own MODE E
31 * receiver expects.
32 */
