4/*
5 * bind_migrate.h — §1.4 cross-worker kXR_bind secondary migration.
6 *
7 * WHAT: With `listen ... reuseport`, the kernel hashes each of a client's TCP
8 * connections to a worker independently, so a secondary data channel's
9 * kXR_bind routinely lands on a different worker than the session's
10 * primary connection. Response offloading (§1.1–§1.3) requires primary
11 * and secondary on the SAME event loop — the primary's worker writes
12 * response frames directly to the secondary's socket — so a scattered
13 * bind used to fall back to inline primary responses, costing the
14 * multi-substream throughput reuseport was meant to enable. This module
15 * hands the freshly-bound secondary's fd to the session-owning worker
16 * over a pre-fork SOCK_SEQPACKET channel pair (SCM_RIGHTS); the owner
17 * adopts the socket into a fabricated stream session and completes the
18 * bind there, restoring same-worker offloading for every substream.
19 *
20 * WHY: reuseport is what spreads simultaneous multi-client accepts across
21 * workers (without it one worker's accept storm serializes everything);
22 * migration is what keeps single-client multi-substream transfers on one
23 * event loop. Together they win both benchmark axes.
24 *
25 * SAFETY: migration happens only at the bind frame boundary — the recv loop
26 * reads exact per-frame byte counts, so no userspace bytes are buffered
27 * past the bind request; anything the client pipelines later sits in the
28 * kernel socket buffer and travels with the fd. TLS secondaries never
29 * migrate (SSL state cannot cross processes) and neither does a
30 * connection with queued responses (replies must not reorder). Every
31 * refusal degrades to the pre-§1.4 behavior: bind locally, respond
32 * inline on the primary.
33 */
