1/*
2 * sss_id.h — per-connection SSS identity registry (the XrdSecsssID role).
3 *
4 * WHAT: A small, thread-safe map from a login id ("lid") to a full SSS entity
5 * (name, vorg, role, groups, endorsements, proxied credential), plus the
6 * bounded setters that fill one such entity and the reader that loads a
7 * proxied credential from a file.
8 * WHY: A multiplexing front end — a FUSE mount under `allow_other`, a gateway,
9 * a batch mover — carries one connection but many callers. Without a
10 * registry every caller's request is minted under the process's own login
11 * name, so the server sees one identity for all of them and per-user
12 * authorization on the far side is a fiction. xrootd solves this with
13 * XrdSecsssID; this is the same contract, clean-room.
14 * HOW: A mutex-guarded dynamic array of fixed-size slots, keyed by an exact
15 * lid string. Lookup is exact and copies out: there is deliberately NO
16 * fallback to "the first slot" or "the process identity", because the
17 * failure mode of a wrong fallback here is one user's request presented
18 * under another user's name. Registration is bounded
19 * (XRDC_SSS_ID_SLOTS_MAX) and every string setter fails closed on a value
20 * that would not fit, exactly like the server's parser.
21 *
22 * The entity itself is minted by the shared kernel (core/compat/sss_entity.c),
23 * so client and server produce identical bytes from one implementation.
24 */
