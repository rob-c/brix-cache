4/*
5 * merge_export.h — the export-anchor sequence every HTTP protocol merge runs.
6 *
7 * WHAT: brix_http_merge_export_anchor() turns a location's configured export
8 * root into the three things the read/write planes below it need: a
9 * canonical path (root_canon), the persistent O_PATH confinement fd
10 * every openat2(RESOLVE_BENEATH) anchors on, and a registered VFS
11 * storage backend. brix_http_register_storage_backend() exposes the
12 * backend plus site-name-translation portion to protocols whose root
13 * preparation has additional ordering constraints.
14 *
15 * WHY: CVMFS, OCI and RPM each ran the identical four steps in their own
16 * merge, in the identical order, with the identical error handling — and
17 * the order is not incidental: the rootfd must be opened on the
18 * CANONICAL path (opening it on the configured one would anchor
19 * confinement on a symlink the operator could later repoint), and the
20 * backend must be registered against that same canonical root or a
21 * remote backend's namespace and the local confinement boundary describe
22 * different trees. A fourth plane copying the sequence would eventually
23 * copy it in the wrong order; there is now nothing to copy.
24 *
25 * HOW: Defaults an unset root to "/" (the pure-cache-node anchor: the location
26 * serves the "/" namespace and fills from its backend into the cache
27 * store), then brix_prepare_export_root → brix_http_open_rootfd →
28 * brix_http_register_storage_backend. Every failure is already reported
29 * by the step that failed, so the caller just propagates NGX_CONF_ERROR.
30 *
31 * NOT in scope, deliberately: the per-plane freshness stamps
32 * (cache_manifest_ttl and friends) and brix_tier_register_stores. Those come
33 * AFTER, and what each plane stamps between them is exactly what makes it that
34 * plane — folding them in would take a policy decision away from the protocol
35 * that owns it.
36 */
